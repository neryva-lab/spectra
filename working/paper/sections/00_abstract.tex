\begin{abstract}
Multi-task learning combines objectives on different numerical scales, making uncertainty-based loss weighting brittle. We propose Bounded Precision-Geometry Scaling (BPGS), a split uncertainty-weighting method that maps each task's latent coordinate through a bounded sigmoid chart and expresses log-variances relative to detached batch log-loss statistics with first-batch calibration. When numerical safeguards are inactive, its normalized weights are exactly invariant to uniform loss rescaling. Across synthetic stress tests, NYUv2 dense prediction, Yeast multi-label classification, and RF1 multi-target regression, BPGS remains strongest on the scale-robustness tests. Under pure loss rescaling from $\times 1$ to $\times 1000$, its macro score changes from 0.777 to 0.778, whereas Kendall weighting drops from 0.780 to 0.637; L1-normalizing Kendall's weights does not recover this stability. On NYUv2, BPGS achieves the best depth absolute relative error (0.223), depth RMSE (0.790), and total loss (1.891), including against Nash-MTL. Controlled studies show low sensitivity to the tested batch sizes and calibration batches, with negligible overhead relative to Kendall. BPGS also achieves the best Yeast micro-F1 (0.616) and remains competitive on RF1 without leading RMSE or MAE. These results support BPGS as a targeted refinement of uncertainty weighting when loss-scale mismatch is the primary concern.
\end{abstract}
